# Quantitative & Technical Analysis Opportunities - 20260628

This document catalogs QA and TA techniques to improve the system's performance toward the high-frequency compounding goal.

## 1. Missing

### High-Frequency Order Flow (HFT-Lite)
- **What**: Analyzing Bid/Ask Delta and Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) at the tick level.
- **Relevance**: Crucial for high-frequency entries. Imbalance in the limit order book (currently implemented) is a static snapshot; tracking the *rate* of aggressive market orders hitting the bid/ask provides much higher signal for immediate direction.
- **Next Step**: Implement a `TradeDelta` indicator that tracks the last 50-100 market trades.

### Statistical Arbitrage (Pairs Trading)
- **What**: Identifying cointegrated asset pairs and trading their spread.
- **Relevance**: Provides market-neutral opportunities. If Asset A and Asset B usually move together, a divergence is a high-win-rate signal for a mean-reversion trade.
- **Next Step**: Perform a correlation scan across the top 250 assets during warm-up.

### Volatility Regime Modeling
- **What**: Using GARCH or simpler standard deviation bands to classify the current market state (Low Vol / High Vol / Expansion).
- **Relevance**: The current 1:2 RRR might be too tight for high-volatility regimes (getting stopped out by noise) and too loose for low-volatility regimes (target never reached).
- **Next Step**: Implement a `VolRegime` classifier to adjust `ATR_SL_MULT` dynamically.

## 2. Misused

### Supertrend as a Hard Gate
- **What**: `RESTRICT_SUPERTREND` blocks trades if they are against the current ST direction.
- **Relevance**: Supertrend is a lagging indicator. In a high-frequency system, by the time the Supertrend flips, a significant portion of the move is often over, or the price is due for a pullback.
- **Next Step**: Move Supertrend from a "Hard Gate" to a "Scoring Component" or use a shorter period for faster reaction.

### RSI Buy Floor / Short Ceiling
- **What**: `RSI_BUY_FLOOR` (15.0) and `RSI_SHORT_CEILING` (80.0) block trades if RSI is too extreme.
- **Relevance**: These are often the moments of highest momentum (the "Moon" or "Flush"). Blocking them prevents the bot from catching the fastest part of a compounding move.
- **Next Step**: Instead of blocking, these levels should trigger a "Momentum Rider" state with tighter trailing stops.

## 3. Underutilized / Not Properly Incorporated

### POI Confluence Score
- **What**: `ta/patterns/poi.py` calculates a `poi_confluence_score` based on OB, FVG, and Liquidity overlaps.
- **Relevance**: Currently, the `LearningModel` barely uses this. It calculates it but doesn't weight it heavily in the final score.
- **Next Step**: Integrate `poi_confluence_score` as a primary weight in `models.py`, requiring a minimum confluence (e.g., > 30) for high-confidence trades.

### Institutional Killzones
- **What**: `ta/patterns/idm.py` identifies London and NY Killzones.
- **Relevance**: High-frequency compounding is most effective when volatility is highest. The bot currently treats all 24 hours equally (except for IDM logic).
- **Next Step**: Implement a `SESSION_MULTIPLIER` for `RISK_PER_TRADE`, increasing exposure during high-volume sessions and reducing it during "Asian range" or flat periods.

### Market Structure Shift (MSS) vs. BOS
- **What**: `ta/patterns/structure.py` differentiates between trend continuation (BOS) and potential reversal (MSS).
- **Relevance**: The bot currently scores both positively. A BOS is safer for high-frequency compounding, while an MSS is a higher-risk "knife catch" or "top pick".
- **Next Step**: Assign different scores/weights to BOS vs MSS. BOS should have a higher confidence score for the compounding goal.
